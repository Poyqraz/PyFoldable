# Printable 13-inch segmented propeller — first manufacturing outputs (2026-11-18)

Generated by `pyfoldable/prototype` (build123d 0.9.1 / OpenCASCADE) from the design records in each folder
(`design.json`). The design and method are in [`docs/prototype_13in.md`](../../docs/prototype_13in.md).

These are assembly and mechanism prototypes. Physical qualification is **false**. Maximum speed, motor, thrust and
printer are not supplied, the material values are exploratory, and every speed below is a what-if speed. Lengths
are in millimetres (STEP declares mm; STL and 3MF are in mm).

## Alternatives (no single optimum)

All four share:

- 330.2 mm diameter; joint plane r_j = 99 mm (proximal 84 mm, distal 66.1 mm);
- NACA2412 printable section with a 0.8 mm trailing edge;
- Ø3 pin, 0.2 mm hole clearance, 0.3 mm lug gap, R_b 5.5 mm, full-depth lugs;
- stop nose and pad ahead of the pin; PLA at 100 % infill.

The pitch quoted is at 0.75R from the twist.

| Folder | What it trades | Offset δ = deployed step | Fold | Lugs prox./dist. | Body clearance (nominal / worst-case play) | Distal / blade mass | Governing check (SF 2) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `A_offset5p5_template_travel50` | Owner's 5.5 mm offset on the template planform (6.3 in pitch); the fold is limited to 50° | 5.5 mm | 50° | 5.42 / 5.58 mm | 1.03 / 0.04 mm | 4.11 / 26.4 g | stop-nose root, ≈ 3220 RPM | passes all checks |
| `B_offset9p5_twist0p6_travel140` | Flatter blade (twist ×0.6, 3.8 in pitch) with a moderate offset | 9.5 mm | 140° | 6.72 / 7.10 mm | 2.18 / 0.64 mm | 4.33 / 26.9 g | stop-nose root, ≈ 3010 RPM | passes all checks |
| `C_offset12p5_template_travel140` | Template planform kept (6.3 in pitch); large offset and step | 12.5 mm | 140° | 8.92 / 9.08 mm | 1.81 / 0.54 mm | 4.60 / 27.7 g | stop-nose root, ≈ 2540 RPM | passes all checks |
| `D_offset6_twist0p35_travel140` | Owner's 6 mm offset on a very flat blade (twist ×0.35, 2.2 in pitch) | 6.0 mm | 140° | 4.58 / 5.19 mm | 0.65 / −0.91 mm | 4.05 / 26.2 g | pin bending, ≈ 3700 RPM | **rejected**: interferes with worst-case play (needs ≈ 7.4 mm) |

In every candidate the 4 mm stop nose doubles as the folded stop at about 144°. A deeper fold needs a shallower nose,
which weakens the stop.

The joint play is large: a tilt of 3.1° (limited by g/R_b) gives a 3.2–3.9 mm droop at the tip. Tightening the gap
and the hole fit after the coupon test is the first improvement.

## Offset needed for the full fold (body clearance; re-checked at the rounded value)

| Twist scale (pitch at 0.75R) | Clear fold at 5.5 mm | Offset needed (nominal) | With worst-case play |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.00 (6.3 in, template) | 57° | 11.2 mm | 12.5 mm |
| 0.80 | 61° | 9.5 mm | 11.0 mm |
| 0.60 (3.8 in) | 67° | 7.8 mm | 9.4 mm |
| 0.45 | 74° | 6.6 mm | 8.2 mm |
| 0.35 (2.2 in) | 82° | 5.9 mm | 7.4 mm |
| 0.25 | 140° (to the required fold) | 5.5 mm | 6.7 mm |

Source: `required_offset_by_twist.json`. The exploration grid (5.0/5.5/6.0 mm × twist 1.0/0.6/0.35, with its
rejection reasons) is in `exploration_offset_x_twist.csv`.

**Conclusion for the owner's 5–6 mm target:**

- On the template blade it clears only about 55–60° of fold.
- Keeping 5–6 mm for a 140° fold needs twist ×0.25–0.35, a very low pitch.
- Keeping the pitch needs about 12.5 mm (deployed step 12.5 mm).

## Contents of each candidate folder

- `step/`: rotor (hub with both proximal segments; placeholder hub Ø30 × 12, Ø5 bore), distal, fit-test pair, hole
  coupon and hardware solids.
- `step/assembly_deployed.step` and `assembly_folded_<n>deg.step`.
- `stl/`, `3mf/`: the printable parts.
- `assembly_drawing.pdf` / `.svg`:
  - sheet 1: plan view with the pin axis, boss and relief, open-stop seat, folded position and critical dimensions;
  - sheet 2: a true section through the pin with planes A and B and the lug, washer, head and clip stack.
- `hardware_list.csv`, `assembly_instructions.md`, and `summary.md` (calculations and assumptions).
- `report.json`, `design.json`, and `SHA256SUMS`.

`browser/` holds screenshots of the *Prototip* page building candidate B, playing its fold, plotting its clearance
and loads, and exporting its package.

The `browser/E_*.jpg` files are the walkthrough of E. It was run in a headless Chrome over the DevTools protocol,
against the local app, because the in-app pane could not render while the app window was in the background. Each
step went through the normal inputs and buttons:

- the deployed and 140° folded 3-D assembly;
- the clearance chart with the governing contact;
- the deployed joint seen from the tip;
- the folded plan;
- the folded-dimension table;
- the measurement-kit section.

The kit zip downloaded there was checked file by file against its `SHA256SUMS`.

## What is committed

Repository size limits what goes into git:

- **Every candidate:** the documents, drawings, design records and `SHA256SUMS`.
- **Candidate B only:** the 3MF meshes and the part STEP files. They were committed as the first-print geometry
  of the first increment. The owner has since chosen B's coupon and fit pair as a measurement prototype only, so
  these files do not select B as the powered propeller.

The STL copies and the assembly STEP files of all candidates, and the geometry files of A, C and D, stay local. The
`SHA256SUMS` lines for those files describe that local generation. To regenerate them, from the repository root with
the `[cad]` extra:

```bash
./venv/bin/python -c "from pathlib import Path; from pyfoldable.prototype import cad, design, export; d = design.from_json(Path('reports/prototype_13in_20261118/A_offset5p5_template_travel50/design.json').read_text()); export.export_candidate(cad.build(d), Path('outputs/prototype_A'))"
```

STEP files carry a write timestamp, so regenerated STEP bytes differ from the listed sums while the geometry is the
same (the tests compare re-imported volumes).

Candidates A–D were generated at commit `8dac1dc`. Two later changes alter regenerated solids slightly:

- evenly spaced loft sections;
- full-depth lugs 0.2 mm proud of the blade.

To reproduce their exact geometry, check out `8dac1dc`. Regenerating them now also gives a different design identity,
because the design record gained fields. Their "with play" required offsets were computed with the 0.5 mm nominal
clearance added. The current rule is the larger of the nominal requirement and zero interference with play. For
example, D needs about 6.9 mm, not 7.4 mm.

## Second increment (same day): offset study, compact candidate E, measurement kit

- `study/offset_study.csv` and `study/study.json`: required offset for each variant at δ = 6 mm, by required fold
  travel, nominal and with the worst-case model play. Each row also gives the mechanism floor and blade term, the
  governing angle and the centrifugal seating at 6000 RPM. The estimates are taken from the gap at 6 mm and are not
  clipped, so a margin shows as a value below 6 mm.
- `study/alignment_envelope_B.png` and `_E.png`, one figure per candidate:
  - the deployed joint seen from the tip (planes A/B, lug bands, pin set);
  - the folded plan envelope with the governing contact;
  - the gap over the travel;
  - the offset needed for each required travel.
- `E_offset6_ts0p86_zone45_travel140/`: the compact candidate, a 13×5.5-class blade (twist ×0.86) with a ±45 mm
  hinge zone (chord ×0.85, twist ×0.45), δ 6.0 mm and a 140° fold. Its full manufacturing outputs follow the
  candidate-folder layout above.
- `fit_kit_B/`: the measurement prototype, with its own README: the hole coupon, B's fit pair at CAD clearances
  0.1/0.2/0.3 mm with a clamp block and a 30 mm load tab, `predictions.json`, `fit_record_template.json` and
  `SHA256SUMS`.

- `fit_kit_E/`: **E's own measurement kit**, the one to print first. δ 6.0 mm, lugs 4.72/5.05 mm, pin 12.5 mm under
  the head, E's stop nose and pad. It has the same layout as `fit_kit_B/`, which stays as a reference.

  Play measured on a kit is direct evidence only for its own design. Applied to another design it is "transferred":
  shown for exploration, while that design's verdict keeps the worst-case model (`docs/prototype_13in.md`).

- `aero/`: the aerodynamic screening of E against the unmodified 13×5.5-class blade
  (`docs/prototype_13in_aero.md`):
  - XFOIL polars of the chord-dependent printable section, with provenance;
  - fail-closed and clamp-diagnostic BEM tables;
  - spanwise loading;
  - `aero_comparison.png`;
  - `equal_thrust.csv` and `compromise_options.csv`: the speed and power for equal thrust at equal inflow, and per
    blade option (reference, F2, F1, E) the offset by fold travel, the folded envelope and the SF 2 speed limit.
    These come from `aero.run_decision`.

  It covers the blade-shape effect only; the joint step is unresolved. Regenerate with
  `./venv/bin/python -m pyfoldable.prototype.aero reports/prototype_13in_20261118/aero`, which needs the pinned
  XFOIL operator bundle.

Candidate E's folded size (all blades, aerodynamic outlines; the stop nose adds about 2 mm ahead of the leading edge
at the joint). The deployed diameter is 330.2 mm, and the 4 mm nose stops the fold at 144°.

| Fold | Envelope Ø | Length × width | Smaller by | Offset needed (nominal / with worst-case play) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 90° | 242.5 mm | 210.8 × 132.2 mm | 27 % | 5.2 / 6.1 mm |
| 110° | 209.9 mm | 209.8 × 126.0 mm | 36 % | 5.6 / 6.4 mm |
| 140° | 206.0 mm | 205.8 × 89.0 mm | 38 % | 5.9 / 7.1 mm |
| 160–180° | about 202 mm | — | 39 % | not reachable with the 4 mm nose |

Beyond about 110° the envelope is set by the joint radius (r_j = 99 mm), not by the folded distal. 140° buys about
4 mm of diameter over 110° and a much slimmer folded rotor (89 against 126 mm wide), and costs 0.3 mm of nominal
offset.

Regenerate all of it with `./venv/bin/python -m pyfoldable.prototype.study reports/prototype_13in_20261118`. It takes
about 12 minutes and overwrites these folders.

## Strengthened F1 candidate (F1S)

- `F1S_sizing/joint_sizing.csv`: the bounded sizing grid on the F1 aerodynamic baseline at δ 6.5 mm. It covers
  pin Ø3/4/5, stop-nose depth 4/5/6 mm, nose extension 2/3/4 mm, and boss R 6.5 mm for Ø4 and Ø5. Each row was built
  and swept in the full assembly and records:
  - every single-lug check's speed limit at SF 2;
  - the folded stop and the clearance needed;
  - the centrifugal seating and stop force;
  - mass and printability.
- `F1S_candidate/`: the chosen joint, with the full manufacturing outputs:
  - parts, assembly STEP and drawing;
  - hardware list and calculation summary;
  - `alignment_envelope.png`, `folded_dimensions.csv`;
  - `operating_envelope.csv`, with the F1 baseline rows for comparison.

  The joint: Ø4 pin, boss R 5.5 mm, nose 4 mm deep and 4 mm ahead of the leading edge. δ is 6.5 mm nominal; with
  the worst-case play model it needs 7.5 mm.
- `fit_kit_F1S/`: F1S's own measurement kit, for its direct verdict:
  - a Ø4.0–4.4 mm hole coupon;
  - fit pairs at 0.1, 0.2 and 0.3 mm clearance with a clamp block and load tab;
  - protocol, predictions and record template.

  E's kit is only for printer calibration.

Regenerate F1S with

## F1S measurement print handoff

`F1S_print_handoff/` is a geometry-and-instructions package for the P1S operator. It has not been sliced in Bambu
Studio. It holds:

- print-oriented 3MF per plate: `01_kupon` first, then `02_cift_c0p00` … `c0p40`, each with named parts;
- STL per part;
- the Turkish guide, the hardware and assembly doc, and the proposed-versus-used form;
- the prefilled measurement record (schema /2), predictions, the design record and SHA-256 sums.

It is generated with `fit.export_handoff(d, folder)`, where `d` is the F1S design from `study.run_candidate`. See
`docs/prototype_13in.md`.

## Print first

F1S's measurement kit `fit_kit_F1S/`, following its README: the coupon first, then the fit pair at the clearance
the coupon picks, with the Ø4 pin set. Enter the record in *Prototip → Ölçüm prototipi*, where F1S is the default
kit, then apply the decision rule in `docs/prototype_13in.md` (*Next full-size prototype*) before printing any full
rotor.

`fit_kit_E/` may be printed beforehand to calibrate the printer. It is not evidence about F1S. The kits are
measurement prototypes; none of them qualifies a powered propeller.
